# Fellowmint: brand and paid-pilot decision

Prepared 2 October 2026. Audience chosen by the founder: students and early-career builders worldwide. This is a recommendation and experiment plan; demand, prices, and revenue targets have not been validated. The existing app has not been renamed and no domain has been purchased.

## Decision

Use **Fellowmint**, with the proposed tagline **Find your next opening.** Start with funded fellowships, research programmes, and open-source programmes for early-career technical builders. Keep global eligibility explicit: nationality, residence, education, and experience constraints matter more than a generic match score.

The name connects fellowships, peers, and growth, is readable as two familiar words, and can extend to grants and other programmes. Its tradeoff is a stronger association with fellowships than jobs. Shortlisted alternatives: Pursuno (shorter, but needs spelling explanation) and Applyrove (clear application association, less distinctive pronunciation).

## Why anyone would pay

[Youth Opportunities](https://www.youthop.com/about) already provides free opportunity discovery. [Opportunity Desk Premium](https://opportunitydesk.org/premium/) advertises personalised discovery, alerts, templates, and other benefits from $1.99/month. Listings and generic AI matching alone are a weak paid proposition.

Test one offer: a **30-day application sprint**, initially **US$6**, with **₹499 as an India-specific price experiment**. These are proposed regional prices, not equivalent exchange-rate conversions or implemented checkout amounts.

Free: a reviewed catalogue, official application links, a basic profile, and a shortlist. Paid pilot: help choosing up to three realistic programmes, an eligibility and evidence checklist for each, a requirements-based preparation plan, and a deadline plan. During the pilot, deliver missing pieces manually and disclose that service clearly. Do not promise acceptance, automatic submission, or expert review that cannot be delivered. Cap support time and measure it before expanding.

The current product can support profile, catalogue, matching, save, and preparation flows. Automated source crawling still returns HTTP 501; freshness therefore requires reviewed imports and manual checks. Existing checkout configuration defaults to a provisional USD9 pass. This plan does not change that configuration, and no live payment transaction has been verified for this launch.

## Distribution: earn access to existing communities

1. Start with five university research, developer, or open-source clubs across at least two countries. Arrange a useful application-planning session and a curated programme shortlist with each organiser. These are proposed partnerships, not existing relationships; outreach requires the founder's authorization.
2. Publish one weekly digest: five funded programmes, with official source, eligibility, funding coverage, deadline, last-checked date, and any uncertainty. Each programme links to a relevant app detail page and a concrete next action.
3. Repurpose that research into two useful LinkedIn posts and one short visual guide per week. Let partners share the relevant edition with their communities. Obtain moderator permission for community promotion.
4. Ask activated users to share a specific programme or shortlist. Reward qualified introductions with a sprint credit only after measuring delivery cost. Track partner source, completed profiles, saved programmes, paid orders, refunds, and application preparation progress.
5. Build search pages from genuinely researched programme and eligibility information. Expand search content after the catalogue stays fresh reliably. Start paid acquisition only after the offer converts and contribution margin can support it.

## First 30 days

| Period | Work | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–7 | Review 30–50 relevant live programmes; interview 20 target users; prepare the paid service; verify payment, fulfillment, cancellation/refund copy, and analytics | Official-source records, recurring user problems, a successful live order and fulfilled pilot |
| Days 8–14 | Run two partner sessions and offer the sprint to qualified users | First five paid customers, recorded preparation progress, support time per customer |
| Days 15–30 | Reach five partner communities, publish four digest editions, and improve the biggest drop-off | Target 100 activated users and at least 10 paid customers; record acquisition source and objections |

These are experiment targets, not forecasts. An activated user has completed enough profile information to assess eligibility and saved at least one relevant programme. If users activate but do not pay, investigate the offer and price before purchasing traffic. If catalogue freshness cannot be maintained, narrow programme coverage before adding more sources.

## Revenue path

200 sprint purchases in a month at US$6 would produce **US$1,200 gross sales** before fees, taxes, refunds, AI costs, and support. At the proposed Indian price, 200 purchases would produce **₹99,800 gross sales**. These are arithmetic scenarios, not recurring revenue or evidence of demand.

For larger revenue, test college or community cohort sales after successful individual delivery. A proposed US$150 pilot for a defined cohort would include onboarding and the same sprint service, with an explicit seat limit and delivery scope. Five such pilots would produce US$750 gross sales. Validate buyer budget, willingness to pay, and delivery capacity before building institutional features. Keep individual profiles private; report aggregate engagement unless individuals authorize sharing.

Provider-paid promotion is a later option: visibly label sponsored placements and preserve independent eligibility and relevance ranking. Pursue it only when useful audience reach exists. The durable advantage to test is fresh, trustworthy eligibility information plus evidence that people move from discovery to preparation.
